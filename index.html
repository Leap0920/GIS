What these numbers can and cannot say. This is one epoch, so the comparison is cross-sectional: hotter and cooler places at the same moment, not a change over time. A significant difference between classes is an association, not proof that planting trees would cool a barangay by that many degrees. The effect size (η²) states how much of the LST variation the classes actually account for — it is moderate, not dominant.
Zonal statistics · 2026 composite

Per-barangay vegetation and surface temperature

Computed from the measured 40 m grid — 16 Sentinel-2 L2A scenes for NDVI, 16 Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 scenes for LST, median composited after cloud masking. Landsat thermal is natively 100 m and is delivered resampled, so a barangay's thermal support is one hectare per native pixel. Barangays below 60 native thermal pixels are flagged rather than reported as reliable zonal means. Click any row to isolate that barangay on the map.

Pipeline & provenance

How a scene becomes a number on this screen

This console is a presentation layer over a measured composite. The rasters were computed offline from satellite scenes and ship as palette PNG overlays plus quantised grids, so every statistic is reproducible from files in this folder.

  1. Acquire

    Sentinel-2 L2A from the Earth Search STAC catalogue, tile T51PTS, bands B04/B08 plus the 20 m scene classification layer. Landsat 8 and 9 Collection 2 Level 2 from the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC API, path/row 116/050, band ST_B10 plus QA_PIXEL. Sixteen usable scenes of each for 2026, all read as windowed cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs — no bulk download.

  2. Mask

    Sentinel-2 pixels outside SCL classes 4, 5, 6, 7 and 11 are dropped. Landsat pixels with the fill, cloud, cloud-shadow or snow bit set in QA_PIXEL are dropped. Masking happens before compositing; masking after it contaminates the median.

  3. Composite

    Pixel-wise median across the sixteen scenes of each sensor. The Philippines is heavily cloud-affected, so a single scene is a cloud map, not a study area. NDVI is computed per scene from B08 and B04 before compositing, then aggregated 4 × 4 to the 40 m grid.

  4. Derive

    LST from ST_B10: digital number × 0.00341802 + 149, then −273.15 for °C. The heat residual is the LST left after regressing LST on NDVI across the study area — positive means hotter than the vegetation cover predicts.

  5. Classify

    NDVI is cut into three vegetation classes at 0.10 and 0.20 — fixed thresholds, not terciles fitted to this dataset, so the classes mean the same thing in another barangay or another year. The sensitivity note on the Relationship page shows that equal-thirds cuts give the same ordering and nearly the same gap.

  6. Test

    Surface temperature is compared across the three classes with Pearson correlation, ordinary least squares, one-way ANOVA, Kruskal–Wallis and pairwise Welch tests with Bonferroni adjustment, on every valid cell. The distributions are computed from scratch in analysis.js — no library, no lookup table — and the same maths runs in stats.js on the server, so the page and the report agree.

  7. Serve

    The raster ships as palette PNGs in tiles/, the values as quantised base64 grids in data.js, and the geometry embedded in the same file (file:// cannot fetch a sibling file). Basemap tiles are the only thing fetched at runtime when the page is opened offline.

SCALE

Resolution honesty

NDVI is natively 10 m, LST natively 100 m thermal. Both are shown on one 40 m grid, so the NDVI layer is generalised and the LST layer is finer than its real support. Any per-barangay thermal statistic inherits the 100 m uncertainty.

LIMITS

What this cannot say

LST is a surface radiometric quantity measured at about 10:15 local, not air temperature. Twelve of the sixteen Landsat dates fall in the dry season, so this is closer to a dry-season figure than an annual one. NDVI explains a moderate share of LST variance; albedo, imperviousness and building density carry the rest.

ACCESS

Who may do what

Served by server.js the console sits behind a login. Viewers read the console and the relationship analysis; analysts also generate the report and export CSV; admins manage accounts and read the append-only audit trail. The browser hides what a role cannot do, and the server refuses it anyway — the button state is a courtesy, the 403 is the control.

FRESHNESS

Why this says “as of”

The layer is dated, not live. Sentinel-2 revisits every ~3.9 days but only ~9% of scenes are under 20% cloud; Landsat thermal arrives every ~10 days and the newest pass is often cloud-covered. Rebuilding the composite is a batch job, so the honest label is the newest scene date, shown in the top bar and repeated in every report.

BOUNDARIES

Where the polygons come from

Twenty barangay boundaries from OpenStreetMap admin_level=10 relations, ODbL. Community-mapped, not the PSA legal boundary set — cross-check against PSGC before printing maps.

This folder carries measured data, not a placeholder. The rasters are a 2026 median composite of real Sentinel-2 and Landsat scenes over Novaliches. They are still a single epoch, so the console shows no trend and no change layer; a second epoch is what those views would need.
