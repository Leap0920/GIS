Pipeline & provenance

How a scene becomes a number on this screen

The console is a presentation layer. Every value it shows is produced offline and served as a static artefact — no correlation is computed in the browser.

  1. Acquire

    Landsat Collection 2 Level 2 surface reflectance and surface temperature, restricted to the dry season (January–April) to reduce cloud contamination. Scenes are pulled through Earth Engine rather than downloaded per-scene from the USGS archive.

  2. Mask

    Cloud, cloud shadow, cirrus and dilated cloud are removed with QA_PIXEL before any compositing. Masking after compositing contaminates the median.

  3. Composite

    Per-epoch median composite, with a minimum clear-pixel threshold enforced per epoch. Epochs with insufficient clear observations are rejected rather than interpolated.

  4. Derive

    NDVI from SR_B5 and SR_B4. LST from ST_B10 (Kelvin, scale 0.00341802, offset 149), or from an NDVI-threshold emissivity with a statistical mono-window retrieval where the stock product has ASTER GED gaps.

  5. Analyse

    Correlation, regression, land-use stratification and spatial clustering, computed offline. Results are exported as static JSON; rasters are exported as pre-rendered XYZ tiles so the console works without a live session.

  6. Serve

    Tiles and statistics are read-only. This mockup replaces both with a procedurally generated raster so the interface can be reviewed before any satellite data is processed.

SCALE

Resolution honesty

Thermal band 10 is natively 100 m. Delivered at 30 m. Any per-barangay thermal statistic inherits the 100 m support, and small barangays inherit the uncertainty that comes with it.

LIMITS

What this cannot say

LST is a surface radiometric quantity, not air temperature. Comparison against station air temperature is indicative only. NDVI alone explains a moderate share of LST variance; albedo, imperviousness and building density carry the rest.

VALIDATION

Reference points

Direction and rough magnitude are checked against published Quezon City and Metro Manila results: Alcantara et al. (2019), Landicho & Blanco (2019), Almadrones-Reyes & Dagamac (2023).

This file contains no satellite data. The raster, the correlation, the zonal means and the trend line are generated procedurally from a fixed seed so the interface can be reviewed. The statistics are computed from that generated raster, which makes them internally consistent — and completely unrelated to Quezon City. Nothing here is a result. Roads, waterways and park outlines are schematic and are not survey geometry.
